# The Burton Inn

**A Five-Year Lease and a Path to Purchase — prepared for the building owner**
**Draft v4.0 — November 13, 2026 — replaces the July 2026 version**

> *A summary of the proposed lease: how the rent works, what the owner receives, and how the purchase price would be set. The projected cost of running the inn is itemized in the attached Exhibit D. Figures are projections from the stated assumptions, not guarantees.*

## The Five-Year Path

| When | What happens | For the owner |
|---|---|---|
| **At signing** (late 2026) | An independent appraiser values the building as it stands today. The lease is signed. | Taxes, insurance, and upkeep move to the tenant the following month. |
| **Years 1–3** (through 2029) | The rooms open first; the kitchen and bar open about halfway through Year 1. | Rent each quarter and a revenue report each month. |
| **Value review** (spring 2030) | The purchase price is set from the appraisal and the three-year record (Section 4). | A price set by an agreed method. |
| **Years 4–5** (2030–2031) | The tenant arranges financing and completes the purchase: the inn alone, or with the Yellow House. | Rent continues until closing. |

## 4 · Setting the Purchase Price

The price comes from a method agreed now and applied at the value review in spring 2030.

- **Step 1 — Today's value.** At signing, an independent appraiser both sides accept values the building as it stands, before the tenant's fit-out.
- **Step 2 — Inflation.** At the value review, that value is adjusted for inflation.
- **Step 3 — Share of proven rent.** The rent earned in Lease Years 2 and 3 is converted to a value at a rate agreed now. If that value is higher than the adjusted appraisal, half of the difference is added to the price, up to a cap of 10% of the adjusted appraisal.
- The tenant's fit-out is not added to the price. The price does not change after the value review.

| If the rent-based value is... | The price is the adjusted appraisal... |
|---|---|
| At or below the adjusted appraisal | With nothing added |
| 12% above | Plus 6% (half of the difference) |
| 30% above | Plus 10% (half would be 15%; the cap applies) |

If included, the Yellow House is appraised the same way (today's value, adjusted for inflation), with no rent-based share.

## 6 · If the Inn Is Still Growing

A Cornell study of 3,699 hotels found that occupancy takes about 3.1 years on average to stabilize. With the rooms opening in 2027 and the kitchen mid-year, the value review will see about two and a half years of rooms and less of the kitchen. Proposed: if the purchase has not closed by the end of Year 5 and the tenant is in good standing, the tenant may extend once, for up to two years, on the same terms and at the price already set.
